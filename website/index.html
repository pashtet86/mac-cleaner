What it looks at

Only sections with something on your Mac show up. If you don't use Docker, you won't see Docker.

Xcode
Build products, previews, device symbols and archives.~/Library/Developer/Xcode/DerivedData
Simulators
Runtimes you no longer target and devices whose runtime is already gone.
Packages and toolchains
npm, Yarn, Bun, pnpm, CocoaPods, Gradle, Cargo, Maven, Go, pip, Homebrew, Metro and Expo caches.~/.npm, ~/.gradle/caches, ~/.cargo/registry
IDEs
Android Studio and the Android SDK — NDK versions, system images, build tools — plus JetBrains IDEs, VS Code, Cursor and Zed.
Docker
Build cache, stopped containers and unused images. Volumes are never touched.
Your files
Large files, byte-identical duplicates, and a map of any folder you want to understand.
Applications
Every app with its size and when you last opened it.

How it stays out of trouble

  • Nothing is deleted. Everything you clean goes to the Trash, so you can put it back.
  • You always see the list first — every item and its size — before anything moves.
  • Personal folders stay out of reach. Documents, Desktop, iCloud, Mail, Messages and Keychains are never cleaned automatically, and macOS-protected folders are never even read.
  • No network unless you say so. On first launch you choose whether to share anonymous statistics. What exactly is sent
