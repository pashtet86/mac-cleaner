Disk Cleaner

Privacy

Disk Cleaner works on your Mac. It reads folder sizes to show you what takes space, and it moves what you choose to the Trash. None of that leaves your computer.

Anonymous statistics, only if you agree

On first launch Disk Cleaner asks whether you want to share anonymous statistics. If you say no, it never connects to our server. You can change your answer any time in Settings.

If you say yes, it sends:

  • a random ID created on your Mac, so one install is counted once;
  • the Disk Cleaner version, the macOS version, and whether the Mac has Apple silicon or an Intel chip;
  • that the app was opened, at most once a day;
  • after a cleanup, which section it was in and how many bytes were freed.

Checking for updates to your developer tools

The Environments page works on your Mac until you press Check Online. Then it downloads public release schedules from endoflife.date and nodejs.org and compares them with your installed versions on your Mac. Your versions are not sent; those sites only see an ordinary download request.

What is never sent

  • file or folder names, paths, or their contents;
  • your name, your Mac's name, your Apple ID or email;
  • your IP address — our server does not store it.

Where it goes

Statistics go to our own server on Cloudflare and are used only to count installs and see which features help. They are not sold or shared. Turning sharing off in Settings also deletes the random ID on your Mac, so a later opt-in starts as a new, unlinked install.
